Lowering Credit Card Annual Fees

September 13, 2026 by Worldly Express

Annual fees are the biggest and most eye-popping number when signing up for a new credit card. Who wants to pay almost $900 a year on a single credit card, much less than 4 of them...

Over my years of studying this game, perusing Flyer Talk, Reddit threads, blogs and Discord servers, I have come across a few techniques, that can be used to help reduce the annual fee on a card. In this space, nerfs could happen almost at any time so it is important to refresh this post before you go ahead and carry out some of these techniques. Credit cards often change their code overnight with no notice. Please email worldlyexpressmail@gmail.com for any data points or updates regarding techniques mentioned in this article.

Effective Annual Fee


Effective annual fee is a concept for how much you would really pay for the card. Certain perks like statement credits, complimentary memberships, and even lounge access can often times be assigned a monetary value. To lower the annual fee, the question would be "How much you would pay for them without holding the card?". Say you normally spend $20 per month on Ubering to get drinks, then the $20 Uber cash from the Amex Platinum card is pretty much worth face value. What if you normally paid for Uber One? Amex advertises that benefit as $120 a year, but the annual subscription only costs $96. In this case, only $96 should be subtracted from the annual fee because that is the amount you would've normally paid anyway. What's left after subtracting all the benefits and perks is the "effective annual fee".

I want to caveat the point of effective annual fee because often times monthly or even annual credits could make us over spend. Would you have gone out of your way every month to eat at Five Guys or would you have cooked at home? Could you have bought an Uber gift card from Costco for 10% off instead of paying directly with Uber cash from the Amex cards? There are a ton of factors that goes into play when calculating effective annual fees and each case is circumstantial.

Unconventional Statement Hacks

This next section goes over tricks that do not involve a full refund but may be cases where you might not be thinking of using the credit. There are credit hacks that you might see on other blogs but are not listed here. These techniques are always subject to change without official notice and are not explicitly listed in the terms and conditions sometimes. The tricks below will help you use the credits more effectively or delay and stack up monthly credits for a bigger purchase. For the credits not listed in this section, there may be a better method listed in the next section, which is why it is not listed in this section.

Dining

Many credit cards offer dining credits at restaurants in general or specific restaurants.
Method: Buy a restaurant gift card online when the merchant processor is "Toast" or buy a gift card in person.

  • Amex Platinum: $100 quarterly (Resy)

  • Amex Delta Reserve Business: $20 monthly (Resy)

Other Merchants

There are other merchants that will allow you to trigger the statement credit through gift card purchases. For Staples and Office depot, the credit triggers on purchasing gift cards for any merchant.
Method: Buy gift cards in person.

Refundable Statement Credit

This last section basically outlines the free money that credit cards will give you because of a lapse in their code. The section builds upon the last section but goes a step further by effectively not paying anything for the statement credit at the end of the month. The concept behind this section is that certain credit card statement credits are not clawed back when the qualifying purchase made to trigger the credit is refunded. For example, you buy a fully refundable airline ticket with 3 checked bags, wait for the Airline Incidental Fee credit to post, then cancel the airline ticket. The ticket is refunded to your credit card but airline fee credit does not get clawed back. In this case, you would end up with a negative balance of however much the airline incidental fee credit. I have been the most successful testing with refundable airline and hotel bookings as they are the easiest to post refundable charges on credit cards to trigger credits. Specifically for hotel statement credits, there are cards that market a "hotel statement credit". In reality, they are often "discounts" when for the booking. As such, refunding a booking will not leave a statement credit behind. These credit cards will be omitted from this list. The following sections contain cards that I personally tested (confirmed), cards I haven't explicitly tested but should work unless proven otherwise (Untested), and methods that have been proven to not work after getting nerfed (Expired).

Airline Incidental

This section covers credits that can only be used to cover specific airline incidentals, like checked bags, seat selections, etc...
Method: Buy a refundable ticket, purchase enough checked bags to trigger the entire credit, wait for credit to post, finally refund the ticket.
